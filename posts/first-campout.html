Hazel's First Campout

We adopted Hazel on January 24th, 2026 — an Australian cattle dog mix with just a dash of husky, coming up on two years old and already full of opinions. When her first camping trip came around, we chose Nine Mile Canyon, Utah, mostly because we could drive most of the way and the scale felt right for a brand-new adventure dog.

Hiking to the petroglyphs

Nine Mile Canyon is famous for one thing: rock art. Miles of ancient petroglyph panels stretch along the canyon walls, and Hazel handled the hiking like she'd read the map beforehand. She is a genuinely good hiker — small enough to feel sure-footed, athletic enough to hurdle logs like a champion. The petroglyphs themselves she assessed once, decided they smelled like rocks, and moved on. Same, honestly.

The thunderstorm

The storm hit us on the way back. One of those sudden Utah flashes of thunder that doubles over the whole canyon — and truthfully, Hazel handled it better than we feared for a dog we'd had for barely six months. She pressed close, kept her head low, and rode it out with the patience she's slowly building.

When she is rattled, though, you know it. Her bark is not a normal bark — it's a long, drawn-out vocalization, halfway between a coyote and a husky, and it can carry across camp in a way that makes you understand every western about coyotes at dusk.

What stuck from the first trip

  • A new dog needs the leash habits set before anything else. We're on-leash every second at camp until we know the place.
  • High-energy cattle dogs still get the zoomies at two years old. A long afternoon walk is not optional — it's the difference between a calm evening and a dog re-decorating the campsite.
  • Settle somewhere with a view. She quieted down much faster once she could see everything at once.

Nine Mile Canyon told us two things: she's a trail dog, born for this, and we'd need a whole system for camping with her. That system became its own field guide.
