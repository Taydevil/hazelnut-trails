What's in Hazel's Duffel

People assume a camping dog means a trunk full of dog gadgets. Hazel's kit is actually small, and every item earns its place by doing double duty. Here's what we actually pack for her.

The three permanent items

  • Her own backpack. She carries her rain jacket and a sweater in it. It trims her load to what matters and gives her a job — a cattle dog with a task is a cattle dog who's happy.
  • One collapsible water bowl. On backpacking trips the same bowl that waters her also feeds her. One thing, two jobs, none of the extra weight.
  • The long leash. She opens up a little at camp but is always leashed — a long leash for camp, and the regular leash for everywhere else. Non-negotiable, even in places that feel empty.

What we learned to leave at home

We don't chase a big list of gear anymore. A tired, well-managed dog needs a lot less than Pinterest suggests. What we spend our energy on instead: enough walking, especially while she's still young and the zoomies come uninvited, and enough space from the things that wind her up. Because the gear that actually keeps camp calm is the stuff you never see — the plan, the leash, and the patience.

What's really in the bag

So, seriously: the pack, the bowl, the leashes, her rain jacket, and a sweater. That's the whole list. Add one nervous-but-sweet cattle dog who warms up into a little oven under the blankets, and you've got the full Hazelnut kit.

Pack the quiet, pack the clean, pack the safe — everything else is just more carrying uphill.
