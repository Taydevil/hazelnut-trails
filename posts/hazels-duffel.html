What's in Hazel's Duffel

People always ask what we pack for Hazel on a trip. The honest answer: more than we pack for ourselves. Here's the running list, refined across a full summer of car-camping trips.

The non-negotiables

  • Collapsible water bowl. Two, actually — one for camp, one clipped to my bag for the trail. She will not drink from a cupped hand. She has standards.
  • Her own sleeping pad. Learned the hard way that "I'll share" means she gets the pad and I get the gravel.
  • A long tie-out cable. Freedom near the tent without the tent becoming a trampoline.
  • Two towels. One is never, ever enough.

The extras that earn their spot

  • A lick mat and a frozen snack. Thirty blissful minutes of quiet during morning coffee.
  • A squeaky stick-shaped toy. She abandoned every stick we found on day one; the store-bought one is forever.
  • A tiny first-aid kit. Tweezers, an antiseptic wipe, a bit of light gauze, and a spare nail clipper. Cheaper than a vet stop for a split pad.
  • Boots. She hates them. We bring them anyway for hot trails. She hates them the whole time, which is the point.

What we finally stopped packing

  • The big bowl. She eats faster from the small one. Fewer lawnmower noises, fewer stomachaches.
  • The camp jacket. She is a dog. With fur. She was not cold.
  • Half the toys. One or two favorites beat an armload that all live under the car seats.
Pack the quiet, pack the clean, pack the safe — everything else is just more carrying uphill.

That's the list. If we've saved one future camper from the gravel-bed lesson, this post did its job.
